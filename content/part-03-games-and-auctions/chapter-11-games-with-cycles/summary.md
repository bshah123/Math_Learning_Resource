Section 11 extends combinatorial games beyond acyclic normal play to loopy graphs, cycle mirroring, and weighted cyclic mean-payoff games.

Key ideas:
- Retrograde analysis finds W, L, and D positions via finite-round propagation from terminal L nodes; D nodes allow indefinite avoidance of L.
- Removing 1 or 2 adjacent coins from an $n \ge 3$ circle is a second-player win by splitting the post-move path into two symmetric halves.
- By Ehrenfeucht–Mycielski, infinite mean-payoff games reduce to comparing mean cycle weights under memoryless positional strategies.

Before moving on, make sure you can:
- Trace retrograde W/L/D labeling on a small directed graph with cycles.
- Prove why the second player wins the $n \ge 3$ circular coin subtraction game.
- Compute the optimal positional value of a 3-vertex Max/Min mean-payoff game.
