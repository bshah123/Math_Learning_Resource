Section 10 covers simultaneous-move games, zero-sum minimax solutions, hidden-information poker betting, and cooperative voting power.

Key ideas:
- Iterated strict dominance eliminates strategies that never belong to any Nash equilibrium.
- In a mixed Nash equilibrium, choose your randomization so your opponent is indifferent among their active moves.
- In hidden-hand poker, calling risks $S$ to win $P + S$, fixing equilibrium bluff fraction $\alpha = S/(P+2S)$ and minimum defense frequency $c = P/(P+S)$.
- Shapley–Shubik counts pivotal arrivals across $n!$ permutations; Banzhaf counts critical memberships across winning coalitions.

Before moving on, make sure you can:
- Solve any $2 \times 2$ zero-sum matrix without a saddle point using the exact $(p, q, v)$ formulas.
- Derive $\alpha = S/(P+2S)$ and $c = P/(P+S)$ from expected-value indifference.
- Compute both Shapley–Shubik and Banzhaf indices on a 3- or 4-player voting game.
