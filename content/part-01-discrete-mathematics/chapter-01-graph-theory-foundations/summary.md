Section 1 gives the vocabulary and eight core theorems that support Hamiltonian cycles, graph homomorphisms, coloring, and tree metrics.

Key ideas:
- Handshake lemma forces an even number of odd-degree vertices; Euler trails need 0 or 2 odd-degree vertices.
- Bipartite graphs are exactly those without odd cycles (Kőnig).
- Trees have $n-1$ edges and a unique Jordan center fixed by every automorphism.
- Hall's condition holds automatically on $k$-regular bipartite graphs ($k \ge 1$), splitting them into $k$ perfect matchings.
- Mantel's theorem bounds triangle-free edges at $\lfloor n^2/4 \rfloor$; Euler's formula $V-E+F=2$ rules out planar $K_5$ and $K_{3,3}$.

Before moving on, make sure you can:
- Prove Mantel's bound $\lfloor n^2/4 \rfloor$ using Cauchy-Schwarz on vertex degrees.
- Show why $K_5$ fails $E \le 3V - 6$ and $K_{3,3}$ fails $E \le 2V - 4$.
- Compute closed walk counts using $\operatorname{trace}(A^k)$.
