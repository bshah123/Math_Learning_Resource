Section 9 establishes Bertrand's postulate and prime counting bounds via elementary prime-power accounting in $\binom{2n}{n}$.

Key ideas:
- Legendre's formula $v_p(n!) = \frac{n - s_p(n)}{p-1}$ and Kummer's base-$p$ carry rule govern factorials and binomial coefficients.
- For $n \ge 5$, primes in $(2n/3, n]$ drop out of $\binom{2n}{n}$, while primes in $(n, 2n]$ appear exactly once, enabling Erdős's contradiction for $n \ge 468$.
- Bertrand directly proves $n!$ is never a perfect power ($n \ge 2$), $H_n$ is never an integer ($n \ge 2$), and $[2n]$ partitions into prime-sum pairs.
